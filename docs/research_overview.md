# Research overview

The project progresses from implementing four strategic-agent architectures to asking increasingly demanding questions about what their scores mean.

1. Direct LLM, hierarchical rule-controlled, hierarchical learned IPPO, and locally trained LLM policies establish executable architectures.
2. Exact regret and action-distribution diagnostics reveal that low scalar regret can coexist with nearly constant behavior.
3. Frozen opponent populations test average and tail performance under distribution shift.
4. History interventions separate behavioral sensitivity from useful strategic inference.
5. Lossless state transformations reveal a replicated, family-bounded representation effect.
6. Model-independent competence gates reject environments that fail to demand their intended capability.
7. OpenSpiel utilities move evaluation toward established imperfect-information games.
8. Logged negotiation data exposes the unresolved gap between teacher-forced prediction and closed-loop population validity.
